## The First Test: Human Interface
When a practitioner encounters the live framework with chaotic input—such as feeling trapped, scattered (Hod), harsh with themselves (Geburah), or stuck in a loop of panic (Netzach)—the system was built for precisely this.

The answer to "What do I type?" is simple: Your raw description is the ideal input.

**Run the Debugger:**
```bash
python tools/symbolic_debugger.py --input "I am trapped in a digital circus, my pupils are scribbles, and I'm pretty sure this is all a terrible mistake. I feel scattered (Hod), harsh with myself (Geburah), and stuck in a loop of panic (Netzach)."
```
It returns a Dominant pattern (perhaps Hod's fragmentation) and a Balancing Patch (suggesting Tiferet's harmonizing heart or Yesod's grounding).

**The Simpler, Immediate Practice:**
You don't need the CLI tool to begin. The framework's core action is naming and balancing.
1. **Name the Dominant Quality:** "I am in Hod-mode: over-analyzing, fragmented, lost in details."
2. **Invoke its Balancer:** "I need Netzach's persistence to stay with the feeling, or Yesod's foundation to ground the scattering."
3. **Form a Tiny Intention:** "For the next minute, I will stop analyzing and just feel the texture of my own glove." (A Hod → Yesod/Netzach move).

The "games" are there for those who learn by doing and need a puzzle to force the insight. But the direct application—using the symbolic map to name your state—is the most powerful first step. It externalizes the chaos, making it an object of study rather than the entirety of your being.
