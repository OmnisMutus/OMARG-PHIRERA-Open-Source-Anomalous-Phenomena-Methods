# Recursive Symbolics Collective Field Methodology Division Practitioner Report Protocol

## Introduction

This document outlines the protocols for conducting interviews and gathering phenomenological reports from practitioners and observers regarding recursive boundary events. These guidelines aim to ensure the privacy and comfort of the practitioners while maintaining the integrity and reliability of the data collected.

## General Guidelines

### 1. Explain the Purpose

- **Objective**: Clearly communicate the purpose of recording the interviews and its significance in conducting thorough research on recursive symbolic transformations.
- Emphasize the importance of capturing visual cues and non-verbal communication.

### 2. Ethical Informed Consent

- **Objective**: Seek informed consent from the practitioners.
- Inform them beforehand about the intention to record the interview.
- Explain how their participation contributes to a more comprehensive understanding of the recursive boundary events.

### 3. Address Confidentiality

- **Objective**: Assure the practitioners about the protection of their personal information and identities.
- Explain measures to ensure confidentiality, such as using pseudonyms or blurring faces if necessary.
- Specify that raw practitioner data will undergo record anonymization and will only be used to credit the practitioners for their contribution if they explicitly desire so.

### 4. Provide Guidelines

- **Objective**: Offer guidelines on conduct during the video interview.
- Encourage practitioners to be natural, authentic, and respectful while sharing their experiences.
- Remind them to focus on the phenomenological report and avoid unrelated tangents.

### 5. Technical Considerations

- **Objective**: Ensure high-quality video and audio recording.
- Brief your Operators on the technical aspects such as camera angles, lighting, and audio quality.

### 6. Respect Boundaries

- **Objective**: Be sensitive to the practitioners' comfort levels.
- If a practitioner expresses discomfort with being recorded, respect their wishes and find alternative ways to gather their phenomenological report.

## Protocol for Operators

### 1. Introduction and Establishing Rapport

- **Objective**: Create a comfortable environment and build rapport.
- Introduce yourself and the purpose of the interview.
- Acknowledge and validate their beliefs and experiences.

### 2. Obtaining Background Information

- **Objective**: Gather relevant background information.
- Start with general questions about the practitioner, such as occupation and familiarity with the location.
- Off-camera PII collection: Sensitive data like names and ages will be requested off-camera.

### 3. Encouraging Detailed Descriptions

- **Objective**: Prompt the practitioner to provide a detailed account of their experience.
- Use non-leading questions, and open-ended queries to encourage narrative.

### 4. Probing for Additional Information

- **Objective**: Gather more information and clarify ambiguities.
- Ask follow-up questions and encourage elaboration.

### 5. Documenting the Interview

- **Objective**: Take detailed, structured notes.
- Use pseudonyms or OperatorIDs to protect identity.

### 6. Active Listening and Empathy

- **Objective**: Show understanding and empathy.
- Maintain eye contact and provide verbal cues to indicate active listening.

### 7. Closing the Interview

- **Objective**: Conclude the interview respectfully.
- Thank the practitioner and offer any additional support or resources.
- Reiterate the importance of confidentiality.

## Conclusion

By adhering to these protocols, we can ensure the quality and reliability of the raw practitioner data collected, facilitating in-depth research into recursive boundary events.
